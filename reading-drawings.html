How to read an engineering drawing

A drawing is a set of instructions for making a part. Use this checklist every time you open one of the drawings in these tutorials.

1. Start with the title block

Bottom-right corner. Before looking at the part, find:

  • Part name and number: what you're looking at.
  • Units: inches or millimeters. Every number on the sheet depends on this.
  • Scale: 1:1 is full size, 1:2 is half size. Never measure the paper; read the dimensions.
  • Material, general tolerances, and the projection symbol.

2. Figure out the views

US drawings use third-angle projection: imagine the part inside a glass box and you're looking at it from outside.

  • The front view is the main one, usually showing the most detail.
  • The top view sits directly above the front view.
  • The right side view sits directly to the right of the front view.
  • Views line up: a feature's width matches between front and top; its height matches between front and side.
  • An isometric view (3D-looking) is there to help you picture it, and usually isn't dimensioned.

Tip: pick one feature, like a hole, and find it in every view. That's how you build the 3D shape in your head.

5. Check your understanding

  • Can you state the overall length, width, and height?
  • Can you find every hole in every view and say its size and whether it goes through?
  • Could you build the part from only this drawing, without the 3D model?
